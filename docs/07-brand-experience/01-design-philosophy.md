# Design Philosophy

## Experience statement

The platform should feel like a trusted treasury workspace for high-value financial decisions, not an administrative dashboard.

It must combine Environment Fund identity with disciplined financial-product design: calm surfaces, precise hierarchy, prominent monetary information, visible workflow, and clear decision actions.

## Design qualities

### 1. Financial clarity

Amounts, rates, tenors, maturity dates, selected offers, and approval status receive the strongest hierarchy. Decorative content never competes with financial data.

### 2. Decision orientation

Every operational page must answer:

- What requires attention?
- What is the current financial position?
- Where is the request in the workflow?
- What decision or action is required next?
- What is the consequence of that action?

### 3. Executive calm

Use generous whitespace, restrained color, subtle borders, limited elevation, and compact but readable information density.

### 4. Enterprise confidence

The interface must communicate control, traceability, governance, and auditability through visible owners, timestamps, approval history, attachments, and workflow stages.

### 5. Arabic-first precision

Arabic is the primary interface language. Labels, actions, statuses, tables, charts, step flows, and navigation must be designed in RTL rather than mechanically mirrored after implementation.

### 6. Progressive disclosure

Show essential decision information first. Place detailed liquidity information, offers, evaluation, approvals, execution records, attachments, and audit history in clearly named sections.

## Visual hierarchy

Use this priority order:

1. Page purpose and required action.
2. Financial value and status.
3. Workflow position and responsible role.
4. Exceptions, risks, deadlines, and maturity alerts.
5. Supporting details and history.

## Prohibited visual directions

- generic admin-template appearance;
- colorful KPI-card grids without decision value;
- glassmorphism or translucent surfaces;
- oversized rounded cards;
- strong shadows or floating panels everywhere;
- gradients not present in approved brand assets;
- excessive badges;
- decorative icons without functional meaning;
- environmental stock imagery or leaf clichés;
- dense patterns behind working content;
- hidden primary actions or ambiguous button text.

## Definition of an attractive platform

Attractiveness is achieved through proportion, hierarchy, typography, spacing, meaningful brand moments, realistic data, and polished interactions—not through visual noise.

The platform should remain impressive after the decorative elements are removed because its structure, clarity, and information design are strong.
